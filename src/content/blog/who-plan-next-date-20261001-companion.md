      <h2>Planning is invisible labor too</h2>
      <p>Someone finds the restaurant, checks the hours, books the table, and remembers the anniversary of the first one. That someone is usually the same person every time, and the effort is invisible right up until resentment makes it visible. The poll below lists the fair ways couples share planning. Taking turns is simplest and most equal. Planning together suits couples who enjoy the anticipation as part of the date. Alternating surprises keeps delight alive while sharing load. Whatever you pick, name it out loud. Unspoken planning defaults are how one partner becomes the relationship&#x27;s unpaid event manager.</p>
      <div class="article-callout"><strong>A gentle rule:</strong> the planner gets pampered on the date. Effort earns appreciation, not just attendance.</div>
      <h2>Four planning systems that share the load</h2>
      <ul><li>Strict alternation: your month, my month, no exceptions</li><li>Inspiration rule: whoever feels the spark plans, guilt-free</li><li>Joint planning dates: a monthly coffee to design the next month</li><li>Surprise rotation: alternate who delights whom</li></ul>
      <h2>Make it yours</h2>

It proves the relationship is something you build together, not something one of you performs for the other.</p>

      <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
      <h3>Are phones really the problem on date night?</h3><p>They are the visible part of it. A phone lying face down is a decision; a phone checked repeatedly is information about how the evening is landing. Worth noticing which of the two it is before agreeing any rules.</p><h3>Does date night have to cost money?</h3><p>No. The point is a protected change of context, and a walk with both phones out of reach delivers that as reliably as a booked table. Spending usually impresses the person paying for it more than the couple.</p><h3>How do you decide whose turn it is to plan?</h3><p>Both names on the plan, one owner per night. Rotating ownership ends the loop of two people who cannot choose a restaurant, and it keeps the work from quietly defaulting to whoever asked last.</p>
