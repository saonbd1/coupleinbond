<h2>Unconditional commitment, conditional behavior</h2>
<p>The healthiest couples separate two things that get blurred in romantic films. They offer unconditional commitment to the person: I am not leaving because you had a bad month, lost a job, or turned out to be flawed in ways I did not foresee. And they hold conditional expectations of behavior: I will not accept contempt, cruelty, or a pattern that damages me, no matter how much love sits behind it. Collapsing those two into one, loving without any conditions at all, is how devotion turns into tolerance of harm. Real unconditional love says both sentences at once: I am here, and here is what I need this to look like.</p>
<div class="article-callout"><strong>A gentle rule:</strong> love the person unconditionally, hold the behavior to conditions. The person is not the behavior.</div>

<h2>The test is ordinary, not dramatic</h2>
<p>You rarely learn what your unconditional love is made of during good months. It shows up when a partner fails at something that matters, when the relationship hits a season neither of you chose, or when you have to forgive something forgivable but genuinely hard. Couples who keep both sentences, commitment to the person and expectations of the behavior, tend to come through those seasons closer rather than diminished.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Does spending more time together build connection?</h3><p>Not by itself. Two people can share a sofa for three hours and exchange nothing. Connection follows attention, so one short undistracted conversation tends to do more than a long evening spent side by side on separate screens.</p><h3>How often should a couple talk about the relationship?</h3><p>Less often than you might expect, but at a set time. A short weekly check-in is easier to keep than daily heavy conversations, and it stops small irritations from stacking up until they arrive all at once. Start with one item under &quot;Four signs love is unconditional and still healthy&quot;.</p><h3>What does emotional availability actually mean?</h3><p>Answering instead of deflecting, and staying present when the topic is uncomfortable. If one partner routinely leaves the conversation, availability is the thing to work on before the subject of the conversation changes at all.</p>
