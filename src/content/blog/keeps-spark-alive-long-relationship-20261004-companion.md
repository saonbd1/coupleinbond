      <h2>Curiosity is the real spark</h2>
      <p>People blame time for a fading spark, but the culprit is usually familiarity without curiosity. You know exactly what your partner will order, say, and do, so you stop asking. The early-days electricity came from discovery, and discovery is renewable. Try the restaurant neither of you can pronounce. Ask the question you assume you know the answer to. Flirt with the history you share instead of mourning the mystery you lost. Vote below for the habit that fits you, then do the smallest version of it this week. Sparks do not need grand gestures. They need new information about a familiar person.</p>
      <div class="article-callout"><strong>A gentle rule:</strong> novelty together beats novelty alone. New experiences bond; separate routines drift.</div>
      <h2>Four spark habits that work</h2>
      <ul><li>One new shared experience a month, however small</li><li>A surprise date with no occasion and no big budget</li><li>Deliberate flirting: compliments, notes, lingering looks</li><li>A shared project with a future: a trip fund, a garden, a skill</li></ul>
      <h2>Make it yours</h2>
      <p>Long love has an advantage new love lacks: a deep well of shared meaning to draw from. Use it. Remind each other of the early story, then write the next chapter on purpose. The spark was never about newness. It was about attention, and attention is always available.</p>

      <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
      <h3>We like each other but feel flat. Is that a problem?</h3><p>It is a signal rather than a verdict. Liking someone and feeling connected to them are different things, and flatness usually means the shared-attention habit lapsed first. Repeat one item from &quot;Four spark habits that work&quot; for three weeks before you judge whether anything moved.</p><h3>Does spending more time together build connection?</h3><p>Not by itself. Two people can share a sofa for three hours and exchange nothing. Connection follows attention, so one short undistracted conversation tends to do more than a long evening spent side by side on separate screens.</p><h3>What does emotional availability actually mean?</h3><p>Answering instead of deflecting, and staying present when the topic is uncomfortable. If one partner routinely leaves the conversation, availability is the thing to work on before the subject of the conversation changes at all.</p>
