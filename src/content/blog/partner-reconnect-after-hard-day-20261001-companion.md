      <h2>Decompress first, debrief second</h2>
      <p>The first twenty minutes after work shape the whole evening. Stress is still high, patience is still low, and one sharp tone can set the mood for hours. That is why the order matters: comfort before conversation. A hug, a glass of water, a change of clothes, and only then the question of how the day went. Couples who build a small landing ritual argue less about nothing, because they stop mistaking exhaustion for attitude. Vote in the poll below for the reset that works in your home, then protect it like an appointment.</p>
      <div class="article-callout"><strong>A gentle rule:</strong> greet the person before the problem. Ten calm minutes together beats an hour of venting apart.</div>
      <h2>Four landing rituals that work</h2>
      <ul><li>A quiet hug at the door before any talk about the day</li><li>A short walk around the block to shake off the commute</li><li>Tea or a snack together while phones stay in another room</li><li>Thirty minutes of solo space, then a real check-in</li></ul>
      <h2>Make it yours</h2>
      <p>Ask your partner which version of you they meet in the evening, tired or tender, and design the ritual around the honest answer. The goal is not a perfect evening. It is two people choosing, night after night, to turn toward each other instead of away.</p>

      <h3>We like each other but feel flat. Is that a problem?</h3><p>It is a signal rather than a verdict. Liking someone and feeling connected to them are different things, and flatness usually means the shared-attention habit lapsed first. Repeat one item from &quot;Four landing rituals that work&quot; for three weeks before you judge whether anything moved.</p><h3>Does spending more time together build connection?</h3><p>Not by itself. Two people can share a sofa for three hours and exchange nothing. Connection follows attention, so one short undistracted conversation tends to do more than a long evening spent side by side on separate screens.</p><h3>How often should a couple talk about the relationship?</h3><p>Less often than you might expect, but at a set time. A short weekly check-in is easier to keep than daily heavy conversations, and it stops small irritations from stacking up until they arrive all at once.
