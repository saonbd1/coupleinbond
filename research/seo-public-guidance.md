# Public SEO Guidance Notes

## Google Search Central: title links

Source: [Influencing your title links in Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)

Google recommends that every page has a title, that titles are descriptive and concise, and that each page uses a distinct title. Google warns against keyword stuffing and repeated boilerplate. Google also recommends making the main visual title clear and prominent, usually with a distinctive H1.

## Google Search Central: snippets and meta descriptions

Source: [Control your snippets in search results](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)

Google states that snippets are created mainly from page content and can vary by query. Google can use a meta description when it provides a more accurate summary. The description must be unique, relevant, and written as a short pitch for the specific page. Google can truncate snippets to fit the device width.

## Google Search Central: structured data

Source: [Introduction to structured data markup in Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)

Google describes structured data as explicit clues about page meaning. Markup must describe visible page content and use complete, accurate properties. Google recommends JSON-LD as an easy format to maintain and advises validation with the Rich Results Test.

## Google Search Central: robots.txt

Source: [Robots.txt introduction and guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots/intro)

Google explains that robots.txt controls crawler access to URLs. It does not reliably prevent indexing by itself. The public site can use robots.txt for crawl guidance while using `noindex` or access controls when a URL must not appear in search.

## Google Search Central: sitemaps

Source: [What is a sitemap?](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/overview)

Google describes a sitemap as a file that lists important pages and related resources. A sitemap can help crawlers discover URLs more efficiently, but it does not guarantee crawling or indexing.
