# Public love-calculator research notes

## Scope

Research uses public search results and public pages. No paid SEO export is connected for this pass. The target is worldwide English-language demand, with trend direction treated as more important than raw volume.

## Source 1: Calculator.net Love Calculator

URL: https://www.calculator.net/love-calculator.html

The page targets the basic “love calculator” intent with two name fields and a 0%–100% result. It explains that the output is for amusement and not a real measure of love. It also adds a short explanation of love, a section about accuracy, example scores, and links to related calculator categories.

Useful content patterns:

- Put the two-name calculator near the top of the page.
- Explain the result before users rely on it.
- Use a clear entertainment disclaimer.
- Add an “How accurate is it?” section.
- Add supporting content about what a love score means and does not mean.
- Use internal links to related tools and supporting pages.

## Source 2: TheLoveCalculator.com

URL: https://www.lovecalculator.com/

The page uses the same broad intent but adds a playful story around the tool. It labels the two inputs as “Your name” and “Your crush,” asks for full names, and uses a direct “Calculate love” action. The page also links to an About page, Love-Links, and Privacy.

Useful content patterns:

- Use human input labels and clear placeholders.
- Keep the first action playful and direct.
- Explain how the tool works in plain language.
- Provide supporting navigation for About and Privacy content.
- Keep the calculator experience separate from the explanation content.

CoupleIn Bond already has a working calculator and Web3 keepsake flow. The strongest content gap is not another generic score claim. It is a helpful explanation page around playful compatibility, name-based scoring, limitations, wallet/NFT keepsakes, and relationship conversation prompts. This can target “love calculator by name,” “love percentage calculator,” “are we compatible,” and related question searches without presenting the score as evidence.

## Source 3: Relate compatibility quiz

URL: https://www.relate.org.uk/are-you-and-your-partner-compatible

Relate frames compatibility as something couples can explore and strengthen, not a fixed formula. Its quiz uses values, effort, communication, and relationship growth as useful reflection areas.

Useful content patterns:

- Add a short reflection section after the playful score.
- Use values, communication, effort, and shared growth as themes.
- Avoid presenting compatibility as permanent or measurable by one number.
- Link calculator results to a practical conversation prompt.

## Source 4: Google Trends comparison

URL: https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&geo=&q=love%20calculator,love%20percentage%20calculator,relationship%20compatibility%20test

Google Trends confirms that the terms can be compared worldwide over a five-year window. The page reports normalized relative interest, not absolute search volume. The focused workbook therefore labels public volume as unavailable and uses keyword variants plus public trend evidence instead of inventing volume numbers.

The loaded Trends page showed these public related-query signals for the comparison: “love calculator by name” was marked rising at +200%, “love calculator by age” at +250%, “love calculator poki” at +750%, and “love percentage calculator by name” at +60%. These are relative trend signals, not monthly search volumes. The “relationship compatibility test” comparison returned zodiac-related queries as top related terms.

## Research conclusion

The calculator page should target the broad tool intent first. It should then capture question intent with sections on accuracy, formula, score meaning, privacy, and compatibility reflection. The blank Quotes, Contact, Valentine’s Day, and About pages should become supporting trust and internal-link pages, not disconnected filler pages.
